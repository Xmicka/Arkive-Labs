# How to Use AI to Outline a Long Article

A long article goes wrong at the outline stage. If the structure is weak, no amount of polish at the end will save it. AI is good at structure, which makes it a natural helper here.

But a normal outline prompt gets you generic headings. The better prompt asks the AI to point out where you need to add your own material.

## The prompt

```
Outline a 1,500-word article titled "[TITLE]". For each section give: a working heading, what the section must do for the reader (one sentence), the real material the author must add that AI cannot supply, and one thing to avoid. Do not include any market rates or statistics. End with the 2 places where a reader is most likely to stop reading and how to fix each.
```

The column that matters is "the real material the author must add". It turns the outline into a list of things to gather before you write.

## The test

I used the title "How to price your first freelance writing project".

![Claude's outline with sections, author material and drop-off points](/images/post27-outline.png)

It returned an introduction, six sections and a close, with a word budget for each (150 for the introduction, 250 for the biggest sections, and so on). For each section it gave a working heading, the job the section does for the reader, the material I'd need to supply, and one thing to avoid.

Some examples:

- For the section on counting hidden work, it said I must add my own tracked time on a past project, including where the surprise was. It warned against a generic task list the reader could have guessed.
- For the section on setting a minimum price, it said to avoid any figures presented as typical, and to use blanks and formulas the reader fills in with their own numbers.
- For the section on negotiating, it asked for one negotiation that went well and one that went badly.

It also named the two places readers would most likely stop: the section where they have to do arithmetic about their own finances, and the checklist section that follows a personal opening. For each it gave a fix, such as opening with a worked example before the formula.

## Why the "author must add" line matters

An AI can write a plausible article on pricing without you. It would be correct and forgettable. The things that make an article worth reading, your own story, your own numbers, your own mistakes, are exactly what it can't supply. By asking it to name them, you find out before you start whether you have enough to write.

If you can't answer the "author must add" line for most sections, you don't have an article yet. Do the work first, then write.

## How I use the outline

1. **Gather the material** for each section before drafting.
2. **Cut sections** where I have nothing real to add.
3. **Draft one section at a time**, from my notes, using the section brief.
4. **Edit by hand** until it sounds like me.
5. **Check the two drop-off points** and make sure those sections open strongly.

## The takeaway

Ask for an outline that tells you what you still need to contribute. That turns AI from a ghostwriter into a planning partner, and it makes it obvious when you don't have enough yet to write something worth reading.
