For many small businesses in Sri Lanka, WhatsApp is where sales actually happen. Customers see a product on Instagram, then message to ask the price, check stock and arrange delivery. Yet a lot of businesses still run this from the owner's personal WhatsApp, mixed with family chats and with no structure.

The free WhatsApp Business app fixes most of that in an afternoon. This guide walks through the setup that matters and the habits that keep it manageable as messages grow.

## Personal WhatsApp vs WhatsApp Business

The WhatsApp Business app looks and works like regular WhatsApp, with tools built for selling: a business profile, a product catalogue, automatic greeting and away messages, saved quick replies and labels for organising chats. It is free to download and use.

Use a **separate number for the business**. It keeps work and personal chats apart, lets someone else take over the phone when needed, and means the number stays with the business if staff change.

## Step 1: Complete the business profile

Customers see your profile when they tap your name. Fill in everything:

- **Business name**, exactly as customers know you.
- **Category**, such as clothing store or restaurant.
- **Description**: one or two sentences on what you sell and where you deliver.
- **Address** if customers can visit, or the areas you serve.
- **Business hours**, so customers know when to expect a reply.
- **Email and website** if you have them.
- A clear **profile photo**, usually your logo.

A complete profile answers basic questions before customers have to ask them.

## Step 2: Build your catalogue

The catalogue lets customers browse products inside WhatsApp. For each item add a clear photo, the name, the price, a short description and, if you have one, a link to the product on your website. Group items into collections such as "New arrivals" or "Gift sets".

Two habits make the catalogue useful:

- **Keep prices and stock current.** An item shown as available that is actually sold out creates frustrating conversations.
- **Share items directly.** When someone asks "do you have this in blue?", send them the catalogue item instead of retyping details.

Customers can add catalogue items to a cart and send it to you as a message, which gives you a clear order to confirm.

## Step 3: Set up automatic messages

**Greeting message.** Sent automatically when someone messages you for the first time, or after a period without contact. Use it to set expectations: "Thanks for contacting Silva Bakery. Our catalogue has today's cakes and prices. Orders need 24 hours' notice. We reply between 8am and 8pm."

**Away message.** Sent outside your business hours, so customers are not left wondering. Tell them when you will reply.

Keep both short and specific. A friendly, clear first reply reduces repeated questions.

## Step 4: Create quick replies

Quick replies are saved messages you send by typing a shortcut, such as `/delivery` or `/bank`. Create them for the questions you answer most:

- delivery areas, charges and timing
- payment options and bank details
- how to place an order
- return or exchange policy
- directions to the shop

Ten good quick replies can save an hour a day for a busy shop, and they make answers consistent no matter who replies.

## Step 5: Use labels to track orders

Labels are coloured tags you attach to chats. A simple set that works for most shops:

- **New enquiry**
- **Awaiting payment**
- **Paid, to dispatch**
- **Dispatched**
- **Follow up**

At the end of each day, filter by "Paid, to dispatch" and you have your packing list. Filter by "Awaiting payment" to find orders that need a reminder.

## Step 6: Make it easy to start a chat

WhatsApp supports click-to-chat links in the format `wa.me/` followed by your full number with the country code and no plus sign, spaces or leading zero. For a Sri Lankan number that starts with `94`. Your app can also generate a short link and a QR code.

Put the link or code:

- in your Instagram and Facebook bios
- on your website's contact page and product pages
- on packaging, receipts and flyers
- in your Google Business Profile

## Step 7: Protect the account

WhatsApp is now a sales channel, so treat it like one.

- **Turn on two-step verification** with a PIN. It stops someone who gets hold of your SIM or a verification code from taking over the account.
- **Never share the six-digit verification code** with anyone. Scammers often pretend to be WhatsApp support or a customer to get it.
- **Back up your chats** so you do not lose order history if a phone is lost.
- **Use linked devices** so team members can reply from a computer without sharing the main phone, and remove devices you no longer use.

**Verify payments before dispatching.** Many small businesses take bank transfers and confirm them from a screenshot of the transfer slip. Edited or fake slips are a known trick. Check that the money has actually arrived in your bank account before sending the goods.

## Step 8: Respect customers' consent

Broadcast lists let you send one message to many contacts, and they are only delivered to people who have saved your number. That is a useful safeguard: it means people who receive your broadcasts chose to keep you in their contacts.

Still, a customer who messaged you once to ask about delivery has not necessarily agreed to receive promotions. Ask before adding people to promotional broadcasts, make it easy to stop receiving them, and keep a note of who opted in. Sri Lanka's Personal Data Protection Act places obligations on businesses that use personal data, including for marketing. ## When to move to the WhatsApp Business Platform

The free app suits most small businesses. Consider the **WhatsApp Business Platform** (accessed through Meta directly or through an approved provider) when:

- several staff need to handle a high volume of chats at the same time
- you want to connect WhatsApp to your website, online store or CRM
- you need automated messages such as order confirmations and delivery updates
- you want reporting on response times and conversations

The platform is billed by message, with prices that depend on the type of message and the customer's country, so check Meta's current pricing and your provider's fees before switching. Moving also changes how you work, so it is worth doing only when the app is genuinely holding you back.

## A daily routine that keeps it under control

1. Morning: check "Awaiting payment" and send reminders using a quick reply.
2. Through the day: reply to new enquiries, label every chat.
3. Afternoon: filter "Paid, to dispatch", verify each payment, pack and dispatch.
4. Evening: mark dispatched orders and update stock in the catalogue.

Set up once, WhatsApp Business turns a crowded inbox into a simple order system. The tools are free. The difference comes from using them every day.
