Many small business websites fail for a simple reason: they look fine, but a visitor cannot quickly tell what the business does, whether it serves them, or how to get in touch. The design gets the attention while the content is left until the last week of the project.

This guide works the other way round. It sets out the pages most small businesses need, what each should contain and the details that are easy to forget. If you are still deciding between a single page, a full site or an online store, start with our guide to [choosing a landing page, website or online store](/blog/landing-page-vs-website-vs-online-store), then come back here to plan the content.

## Start with three questions

Before writing a word, answer these in a sentence each:

1. **Who are we for?** Be specific: "families in Kandy planning a wedding" is more useful than "everyone".
2. **What do we want visitors to do?** Call, send a WhatsApp message, book, request a quote or buy.
3. **Why should they choose us?** Something true and specific, such as fast turnaround, a particular speciality or years in a location.

Every page should support these answers. If a section does not help a visitor understand who you serve, trust you or take the next step, it probably does not need to be there.

## The home page

The home page has one job: tell visitors within a few seconds that they are in the right place, then point them to the next step. Include:

- **A clear headline** saying what you do and for whom. "Interior design for apartments in Colombo" beats "Designing your dreams".
- **A short supporting line** that adds your main reason to choose you.
- **One main call to action**, such as "Request a quote" or "Message us on WhatsApp", visible without scrolling on a phone.
- **A summary of your services or products**, each linking to more detail.
- **Proof that you are real and capable**: photos of your actual work, team or premises, and genuine reviews if you have them.
- **Your location and contact details**, or at least the area you serve.

Avoid sliders that rotate through several messages. Visitors rarely wait for them, and the most important message gets lost.

## Service or product pages

If you offer more than one main service, give each its own page. This helps visitors find what they need and gives search engines a clear page to show for each service. For each page, cover:

- what the service is, in plain words
- who it is for and what problem it solves
- what is included, and what is not
- how the process works, step by step
- a price, a starting price or what affects the price, where you can share it
- examples of the work, with photos where possible
- common questions
- a call to action at the end

Price is the detail businesses most often leave out. Even "packages from Rs. X" or "most projects fall between X and Y" helps visitors decide whether to contact you and saves you time on enquiries that were never a fit.

## The about page

People buy from people, especially from small businesses. An about page does not need to be long, but it should be real:

- how and why the business started
- who runs it, with names and real photos
- what you believe about your work or how you treat customers
- any relevant qualifications, memberships or registrations you can genuinely show

Write it in your own voice. Stock photos of smiling strangers in an office undermine trust rather than build it.

## The contact page

Make getting in touch effortless:

- **Several ways to reach you**: phone, WhatsApp, email and a short form. Make phone numbers tappable on mobile.
- **Your address and a map** if customers visit you, and opening hours that match your Google Business Profile. Our [Google Business Profile guide](/blog/google-business-profile-guide-for-sri-lankan-businesses) explains why consistency matters.
- **A short form.** Ask only for what you need to reply: name, a way to contact them and their message. Every extra field puts some people off.
- **What happens next.** "We reply within one working day" sets expectations and reduces repeat messages.

Test the form yourself after launch, and again every few months. Broken contact forms are surprisingly common and can go unnoticed for weeks.

## Trust pages and legal basics

These pages are not exciting, but their absence is noticed:

- **A privacy notice**, if you collect any personal data through the site, including contact forms, bookings, newsletters or analytics. It should explain what you collect, why, how long you keep it and how people can contact you about it. Sri Lanka's Personal Data Protection Act No. 9 of 2022 sets out what organisations must tell people about how their data is processed. This is general information, not legal advice; check the Act and guidance from the Data Protection Authority of Sri Lanka for your situation.
- **Terms of sale, delivery and refund information**, if you sell online or take bookings and deposits.
- **Business details**: your registered business name and where you are based, so visitors know who they are dealing with.

## Details that are easy to forget

Once the main pages are written, check for these:

- **Mobile first.** Most visitors are likely to be on a phone. Read every page on one before launch.
- **Page titles and descriptions.** Each page should have its own title that says what it is about, such as "Wedding Photography in Kandy | Business Name".
- **Real images, compressed.** Large image files slow pages down, particularly on mobile data.
- **Readable text.** Short paragraphs, clear headings and enough contrast between text and background.
- **Links to your social profiles and WhatsApp**, but not so prominently that visitors leave before they understand what you offer.
- **A working 404 page** that helps people find their way back if a link breaks.
- **Analytics**, so you can see which pages people visit and where enquiries come from. If you use cookies or tracking, mention them in your privacy notice.

## Write the content before the design

The most practical change you can make is to write your content first, even roughly, before choosing a design or a template. Open a document, add a heading for each page above and fill in the bullet points. Then:

1. Ask someone who does not know your business to read the home page and tell you what you do. If they cannot, rewrite the headline.
2. Cut anything that repeats or does not help the visitor decide.
3. Gather real photos for every page.
4. Only then choose a design that fits the content, rather than squeezing content into a design.

A website built this way is quicker to launch, easier to keep up to date and far more likely to turn visitors into enquiries.

## Keep it current

A website is not finished at launch. Put a short review in the calendar every three months: check prices, opening hours, services and staff photos, test the contact form and add recent work. An out-of-date site tells visitors the business may not be active, even when it is busier than ever.
