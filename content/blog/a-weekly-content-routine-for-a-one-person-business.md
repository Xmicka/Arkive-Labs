# A Weekly Content Routine for a One-Person Business

If you run a business alone, content is the job you do after the job. It slips for two weeks, then you post three things in a day, then it slips again. A simple routine, with small blocks of time, works better than occasional bursts.

Here is a routine I'd suggest. It's built around the idea that you should gather real material once and reuse it.

## The principle: one piece of real work, many outputs

Every week, do one thing worth writing about: run a test, finish a project, answer a client question, fix a mistake. Then turn that single thing into several pieces:

- A blog post.
- Two or three social posts.
- One short note for an email list, if you have one.

This keeps the content honest, because it comes from work you actually did. It also saves time. You aren't inventing topics every day.

## The week

**Monday: Pick and plan (30 minutes).**
Choose the one piece of real work to write about. Write rough notes: what you did, what happened, what you'd change. If nothing happened this week, use a question a client asked.

**Tuesday: Draft the main piece (60-90 minutes).**
Write the blog post from your notes. Use AI for structure if it helps, and use a prompt that asks it to work only from your notes.

**Wednesday: Edit and fact-check (45 minutes).**
Read it aloud, cut filler, and check every number and name against a source. This is the step people skip.

**Thursday: Repurpose (30 minutes).**
Turn the post into social posts. Ask the AI to use only what's in the post, then edit.

**Friday: Publish and review (20 minutes).**
Publish, schedule the social posts, and note what you learned for next week.

That's roughly three to four hours. Adjust it to your life, and shrink it before you skip it.

## Keep a running log

The hardest part is remembering what you did. A short note file helps: one line a day about anything you tested, a mistake, a question, or a surprise. When Monday comes, you pick from the log.

## Using AI in the routine

- **Planning:** to turn your notes into an outline.
- **Drafting:** from your notes, not from its memory.
- **Editing:** to find filler and vague claims, as in the proofreading checklist.
- **Repurposing:** to turn one post into several, with the "use only what's in the post" rule.

Don't use it to invent topics or to fill gaps in your experience.

## When a week goes wrong

It will. Have a fallback: a short post from your log, or a repurposed older piece with a new angle. A smaller output is better than none, as long as it is true.

## Measure lightly

Once a month, look at which posts got read or replied to, and which brought an enquiry. Do more of what works. Don't obsess over daily numbers when you're small.

## The takeaway

A routine you can keep is worth more than an ambitious one. Do one real thing a week, write it up, check it, and turn it into a few pieces. Over a year, that adds up to a body of work.
