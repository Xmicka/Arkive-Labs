For a small business selling online, photos do much of the selling. Customers cannot pick the product up, so the photo has to answer their questions: what it looks like, how big it is, what it is made of and whether it is worth the price. Blurry, dark or inconsistent photos make even a good product look doubtful.

You do not need a professional camera or a studio to fix this. A recent smartphone, a window and a few household items can produce clean, consistent photos. This guide sets out a simple routine you can repeat every time you add new stock.

## Start with light, not equipment

Light matters more than the camera. The easiest good light is daylight from a window.

- **Shoot beside a large window**, with the product side-on to the light, not with the window behind it.
- **Avoid direct sun.** Harsh sunlight creates hard shadows and blown-out highlights. A bright overcast day, or a window that does not get direct sun, is ideal.
- **Soften the light** if it is too strong by hanging a thin white curtain, tracing paper or a white bedsheet over the window.
- **Turn off indoor lights.** Mixing daylight with warm bulbs gives photos an uneven colour cast.

Shoot at the same time of day for each session, so your photos look consistent from one batch to the next.

## Build a simple home studio

You need very little:

- **A plain background.** A large sheet of white or light grey card works well. Curve it from the wall down onto the table, so there is no visible line behind the product. This is often called a sweep.
- **A reflector.** A piece of white card or foam board placed on the side opposite the window bounces light back and softens shadows. Aluminium foil on card gives a stronger effect.
- **A stable surface** at a comfortable height, such as a table near the window.
- **Something to steady the phone.** A cheap phone tripod helps a lot. Failing that, rest the phone on a stack of books.
- **Small helpers**: tape, blu-tack and clips to hold products and backgrounds in place.

For larger items like furniture, use a clean, uncluttered wall and floor near a big window instead.

## Set up your phone properly

A few settings make a noticeable difference:

1. **Clean the lens.** Phone lenses collect fingerprints, and a smudge makes every photo hazy. Wipe it with a soft cloth before each session.
2. **Use the main camera** at 1x zoom where possible. Digital zoom reduces quality. Move closer instead.
3. **Turn off the flash.** It creates harsh reflections and flat-looking products.
4. **Tap to focus on the product**, then adjust the brightness slider if the photo looks too dark or too bright. On many phones you can press and hold to lock focus and exposure.
5. **Turn on the grid** in camera settings to help keep products straight and centred.
6. **Use the self-timer** or a remote shutter, so pressing the button does not shake the phone.

Avoid heavy portrait or beauty modes for product shots. They can blur edges in unpredictable ways.

## The shots every product needs

Customers want to see a product from several angles. For most products, aim for:

- **A main shot** on a clean background, showing the whole product clearly. This is the image used in listings and search results.
- **Angles**: front, side, back and a three-quarter view.
- **Close-ups** of texture, material, stitching, labels or any detail that justifies the price.
- **A scale shot**, showing the product in a hand, being worn or next to a familiar object, so people understand its size.
- **A lifestyle shot**, showing the product in use or in a real setting.
- **Variations**: each colour or size option photographed in the same way.

Keep the same background, angle and distance for the main shot of every product. A consistent grid of products looks far more professional than a mix of styles.

## Edit lightly and consistently

Editing should make photos accurate, not dramatic. Customers who receive something that looks different from the photo are more likely to complain or return it.

Using your phone's built-in editor or a free editing app:

- **Straighten and crop** so the product is level and centred.
- **Adjust brightness and contrast** until white backgrounds look white rather than grey.
- **Correct colour** so the product matches what you see in person. Compare it on screen next to the real item.
- **Remove small marks or dust** on the background, but not flaws in the product itself.

If you want a pure white background for marketplaces or a clean website, background removal tools can help. Check edges carefully, especially around hair, fur, fabric and transparent items, where these tools often struggle.

Save your edits as a preset if your app allows it, and apply the same settings to every photo from the same session.

## Size and name your files for the web

Large photo files slow your website down, especially for visitors on mobile data.

- **Resize before uploading.** For most websites, around 1,500 to 2,000 pixels on the longest side is plenty. Check what your website platform or theme recommends.
- **Compress the images** using your platform's built-in tools or a free image compressor.
- **Name files descriptively**, for example `handwoven-cotton-tote-bag-blue.jpg` rather than `IMG_4821.jpg`.
- **Write short alt text** describing each image when you upload it. It helps visitors using screen readers and helps search engines understand the image.

Marketplaces and social platforms each have their own size and shape recommendations, which change from time to time. Check their current help pages before a big upload. ## Make it a routine

Set up a repeatable process so new products are photographed the same way every time:

1. Keep your background, reflector and tripod together in one box.
2. Write down your setup: where the table goes, which window, which phone settings and which editing preset.
3. Photograph new products in batches, on the same kind of day and at the same time.
4. Edit the whole batch together.
5. Upload, check how the photos look on a phone, and fix anything that looks off.

## Common mistakes

- **Cluttered backgrounds** that pull attention away from the product.
- **Shooting in dim rooms** and relying on editing to brighten the image, which makes photos grainy.
- **Inconsistent styles** across products, which makes a store look disorganised.
- **Over-editing colours**, leading to unhappy customers and returns.
- **Using supplier or stock photos only.** They can be useful, but your own photos show customers exactly what they will receive and help you stand out from other sellers using the same images.

Good product photos are a skill you can build in a few sessions. Once your setup is in place, photographing a new product takes minutes, and your listings will look as trustworthy as the products themselves.
