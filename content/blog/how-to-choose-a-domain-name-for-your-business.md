Your domain name appears on your website, your email address, your business cards, your invoices and the side of your delivery van. Once customers know it, changing it is disruptive and expensive. It is worth spending an hour getting it right.

This guide covers how to choose a good name, which ending to use, what to check before buying and how to register it so your business keeps control of it for years to come.

## What makes a good domain name

The best domain names share a few qualities:

- **Easy to say.** If you tell someone your web address over the phone, they should be able to type it without asking you to spell it.
- **Easy to spell.** Avoid unusual spellings, words people commonly misspell, and letters that sound alike.
- **Short enough to remember.** There is no strict limit, but shorter names are easier to type and fit better on signage and in email addresses.
- **Clearly connected to your business.** Ideally it is your business name, or very close to it.
- **Built to last.** Avoid tying it to something that may change, such as a single product, a year or a location you might move away from.

## Avoid the common traps

Some choices cause problems later:

- **Hyphens.** People forget them or type the version without, which may belong to someone else.
- **Numbers.** Saying "four" leaves people unsure whether you mean the digit or the word.
- **Double letters at word joins**, such as a name ending and the next starting with the same letter, which people often miss.
- **Accidental words.** Read the name as one long word to check it does not spell something unintended when the spaces are removed.
- **Names close to another business**, especially a competitor or a well-known brand. It confuses customers and can lead to legal disputes over trade marks.

Say the name aloud to a few people and ask them to write it down. If they get it wrong, so will customers.

## Choosing the ending: .lk, .com or something else

The ending, called a top-level domain, says something about your business.

- **.lk** signals a Sri Lankan business and suits companies whose customers are mainly in Sri Lanka. There are also second-level options like .com.lk. Sri Lankan domains are managed by the LK Domain Registry, which sets the rules, prices and any eligibility requirements. Check its website for current details before deciding.
- **.com** is the most familiar ending worldwide and suits businesses with international customers, or those who expect to grow beyond Sri Lanka.
- **Newer endings** such as .studio, .store or .tech can work for some brands, but customers may assume a .com and end up somewhere else.

Many businesses register both their .lk and .com versions, use one as the main address and redirect the other to it. This protects your name and catches people who guess the wrong ending.

## Check before you buy

Before registering, spend a few minutes checking:

1. **Availability** of the name in the endings you want, using your chosen registrar's search.
2. **Social media handles.** Ideally the same name is available on the platforms you use, so customers can find you consistently.
3. **Existing businesses and trade marks** with similar names, through a web search and, for important brands, a trade mark search or professional advice.
4. **The domain's history**, if the name has been registered before. A previously used domain may have a poor reputation from spam or other misuse. Search for the name and check what used to be on it.
5. **Pronunciation in the languages your customers speak.** A name that works in English may sound awkward or mean something else in Sinhala or Tamil.

If your ideal name is taken, small changes often work: adding what you do, such as "nameinteriors", or your location, such as "namecolombo". Avoid buying a name from a domain reseller at a high price unless it is truly essential to your brand.

## Register it the right way

This step is where many small businesses go wrong, and it can cause serious problems years later.

- **Register it in the business's name**, with the business as the registrant, not a staff member, designer or developer personally.
- **Use a business email address you control** for the registrar account, ideally a shared one such as admin@ rather than a personal inbox.
- **Turn on two-factor authentication** on the registrar account.
- **Enable automatic renewal** and keep the payment card up to date. Expired domains can be lost, and your email and website stop working when it lapses.
- **Turn on the registrar lock**, often called a transfer lock, if your registrar offers it.
- **Keep a record** of the registrar, account login location, renewal date and who has access.

If an agency or developer registers the domain for you, ask them to register it in your business's name and account, and to give you full access. Our [website security checklist](/blog/website-security-checklist-for-small-businesses) explains why domain control matters so much for security.

## Set up email on your domain

Once you have the domain, use it for email. An address like hello@yourbusiness.lk looks far more established than a free email account, and it stays with the business even if staff change.

Most businesses use a provider such as Google Workspace, Microsoft 365 or Zoho for email on their own domain. Each provider gives step-by-step instructions for connecting your domain, which usually involve adding a few records in your domain settings.

## Connect it to your website and the rest of your presence

When your website is ready, point the domain to it following your host's instructions. Then:

- update your Google Business Profile, social profiles and WhatsApp Business profile with the web address
- add it to invoices, quotes, email signatures and printed material
- redirect any other domains you own to the main one

If you are still deciding what sort of website to put on it, our guide to [choosing a landing page, website or online store](/blog/landing-page-vs-website-vs-online-store) can help.

## A quick checklist

- Easy to say, spell and remember
- No hyphens, numbers or awkward letter combinations
- Right ending for your customers, with the main alternative registered too
- Social handles and trade marks checked
- Registered in the business's name, with two-factor authentication, auto-renewal and transfer lock on
- Business email set up on the domain

A good domain name is a small decision that pays off for as long as your business exists. Choose carefully, register it properly, and it will quietly do its job for years.
