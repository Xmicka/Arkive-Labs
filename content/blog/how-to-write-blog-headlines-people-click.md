# How to Write Blog Headlines People Click

A headline has two jobs. It has to make the right reader want to click, and it has to be true to the post. Headlines that only do the first job get clicks and lose trust.

AI is useful here, not because it writes the headline for you, but because it can produce many options quickly and criticise them. Here is the prompt I used and what it showed.

## The prompt

```
Give me 10 headline options for a blog post about [TOPIC]. After the list, critique each in under 10 words (vague, clickbait, promises too much, etc.), then pick the best 2 and say why. Do not use numbers or claims the post could not back up, and no headline may promise a result or a time saving.
```

The critique step is where the value is. Ask a tool to write headlines and it will happily write clickbait. Ask it to judge them against rules and you get something useful.

## The ten options

I asked for headlines on how to use AI to draft a freelance proposal without it sounding generic.

![Ten headline options from Claude](/images/post26-headline-options.png)

Here is a selection of what came back, with the critiques:

- "How to Use AI for Freelance Proposals Without Losing Your Voice": clear and honest, but a little plain.
- "Stop Letting AI Make Your Proposals Sound Like Templates": slightly scolding; assumes the reader's mistake.
- "What Clients Notice When a Proposal Was Written by a Machine": a curiosity hook, but implies knowledge of clients.
- "The Secret to AI Proposals That Never Sound Generic": clickbait; "secret" and "never" overpromise.

It picked two: "How to Use AI for Freelance Proposals Without Losing Your Voice" because it matches the real topic and uses the phrases a freelancer would search for, and "AI Can Draft Your Proposal. Only You Can Make It Yours." because it is the most memorable and promises no outcome.

I agree with both picks and with its rejection of the "secret" headline. I'd keep the first for search and use the second as a social media line.

## Rules I follow

- **Make it true.** If the post can't deliver what the headline says, change the headline.
- **Use the words your reader would type.** For a how-to post, a plain headline with the topic in it usually wins in search.
- **Avoid "secret", "never", "always", "ultimate", "everything".** They promise more than a post can deliver.
- **Don't invent numbers.** "7 ways" is fine if there are seven. A made-up "37% faster" is not.
- **Write two versions.** One for search, one for sharing. They don't need to be the same.

## How to choose

Read each option and ask three questions:

1. Would the right person know what this is about?
2. Could I defend every word of it?
3. Is it different from the ten other posts on the same topic?

## What I don't do

I don't test headlines on a big audience or claim any headline "gets more clicks". I don't have the data to say that. A small blog can't run reliable tests, so I rely on clarity and honesty.

## The takeaway

Use AI to widen the options and to criticise them, then choose the one you can stand behind. A headline that is honest and specific will keep the readers you want.
