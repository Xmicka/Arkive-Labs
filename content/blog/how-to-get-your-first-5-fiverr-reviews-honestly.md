# How to Get Your First 5 Fiverr Reviews Honestly

I am writing this with zero reviews. I have three live gigs and no orders yet, so I can't tell you what worked for me. What I can do is set out the plan I'm following, and be clear about the shortcuts I'm avoiding.

The first reviews matter because a gig with none looks risky to a stranger. That pressure is exactly why people are tempted to cheat.

## The shortcuts to skip

- **Buying reviews.** Sellers offer them. They violate Fiverr's rules, and you'd be paying for something that can get your account removed. Read Fiverr's terms yourself rather than taking my word for it.
- **Ordering your own gig from a second account.** The same problem, and it is easy for a platform to spot.
- **Swapping reviews with friends or other sellers.** Trading is not the same as earning.
- **Pressuring buyers for five stars.** Asking nicely for honest feedback is one thing. Begging or bargaining for a rating is another.

Besides the account risk, fake reviews cost you something else: you learn nothing about whether your work is good.

## What I'm doing instead

**1. Make the first order easy to say yes to.** I've set the entry-level package so that someone can try the service without a big commitment. Make sure you can still do good work at that price. A cheap first order that you resent will show up in the result.

**2. Ask for what you need in the requirements.** Each of my gigs asks the buyer for topic, tone, length and anything to include or avoid. The better the brief, the better the work, and the better the work, the better the review.

**3. Deliver early, and over-communicate.** Send the work a little ahead of time with a short note on what you did and why. Small things, such as pointing out a fact you double-checked, make a buyer feel looked after.

**4. Offer one round of changes and mean it.** Take feedback without arguing. A buyer who feels heard often reviews more kindly, even if you had to redo something.

**5. Ask once, politely, after delivery.** Something like: "If you were happy with the work, a short review helps me a lot. If something wasn't right, tell me and I'll fix it." That sentence asks for honesty, not for stars.

## Fix the reasons people don't buy

While waiting for orders, I'm doing the unglamorous work:

- A profile that says exactly who I write for and what I won't do.
- Portfolio pieces that are clearly labelled for what they are, with no invented clients.
- FAQs that answer the doubts of someone buying from an unknown seller.
- A cover image that looks like a person made it.

## Look beyond Fiverr

Don't make Fiverr the only place a first review can come from. Direct outreach, as in the earlier post on cold emails, can lead to a first paid project whose result you can describe honestly in a portfolio, even if it doesn't produce a Fiverr review.

## What I'll do when I have reviews

I'll update this post with what actually happened, including how long it took and what I'd change. Until then, treat this as a plan, not a record.

## The takeaway

There's no honest shortcut to your first five reviews. Do good work, make it easy for a buyer to take a chance, ask honestly, and remove every other reason someone might hesitate.
