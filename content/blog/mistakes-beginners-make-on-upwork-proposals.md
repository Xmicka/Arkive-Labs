# Mistakes Beginners Make on Upwork Proposals

When you're new on Upwork, every proposal feels like a lottery ticket. Upwork charges "Connects", its credits for applying to jobs, so each proposal costs something. That makes it worth getting the proposal right.

I'm new to this myself, and I haven't won jobs through Upwork yet, so this isn't a list of tricks that worked for me. It's a list of common mistakes, and how I'm trying to avoid them in my own proposals.

## Mistake 1: Opening with yourself

"Hi, I'm a professional writer with five years' experience..." The client's first question is whether you understood their job. Start with something from their post, such as their problem or a detail only someone who read it would know.

## Mistake 2: Sending the same proposal to everyone

Clients can often tell. A proposal that could be sent to any job says that you didn't read this one. Take two minutes to find the one thing specific to this job and build the opening around it. My earlier post on writing proposals with AI shows how to find that detail.

## Mistake 3: Claiming experience you don't have

This is the most damaging one. Don't invent past clients, results or years of experience. If you have none, say what you have: a portfolio piece, labelled honestly, and a clear idea of how you'd approach the job. Honest and specific beats vague and inflated.

## Mistake 4: Writing too much

Most clients skim. Aim for a short proposal: what you understood, one relevant example, a first step, and one question. Long self-descriptions get skipped.

## Mistake 5: Not asking a question

A good question shows you've thought about the project. "Will you send transcripts or should I work from the videos?" is easy to answer and signals that you've considered how the work will go. It also starts a conversation.

## Mistake 6: Ignoring the instructions

Some job posts say "start your proposal with the word X" or "include your answer to Y". They're often a test of whether you read the post. If you skip the instruction, you may be filtered out immediately.

## Mistake 7: Underpricing out of fear

Charging much less than the job warrants can signal low quality, and it can leave you doing too much work for too little. Use your own cost floor, as in my post on pricing. Offering a small first piece or a clearly scoped start is a better way to lower the risk for the client than slashing your price.

## Mistake 8: Letting AI write it unedited

AI drafts are fine as a start, but unedited ones tend to open with stock phrases and make claims you can't back up. Read the draft aloud, cut the filler, and add a detail only you would know.

## Mistake 9: Applying to everything

With Connects costing money, apply where you can credibly do the work and where the client sounds clear about what they want. A smaller number of tailored proposals is usually a better use of Connects.

## Mistake 10: Moving off the platform

If a client asks you to continue by email or another app before a contract exists, be careful. Upwork's rules are meant to protect both sides, and moving away can leave you without protection. Treat unexpected links and requests as suspicious, as I would on any freelance platform.

## A quick checklist before you send

1. Does the first line refer to their job?
2. Is every claim true?
3. Is it short?
4. Does it end with a question?
5. Did I follow every instruction in the post?

## The takeaway

Most of these mistakes come from writing about yourself instead of about the client's problem. Read the post, say what you understood, be honest about what you have, and ask a good question.
