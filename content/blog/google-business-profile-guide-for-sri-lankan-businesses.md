When someone searches "bakery near me" or "phone repair Nugegoda", the results that appear on the map come from Google Business Profiles. For many local businesses, that profile gets more views than the website. It is free, and most profiles we see are either incomplete or out of date.

This guide covers how to set one up properly and the small, regular upkeep that keeps it working.

## Who should have one

You are eligible if you meet customers in person, either at a location they visit (a shop, clinic, restaurant, salon) or by travelling to them within an area you serve (plumbers, tutors, caterers, cleaning services). Purely online businesses with no customer contact in person are generally not eligible.

## Step 1: Claim or create the profile

Go to Google Business Profile and sign in with a Google account the business owns. Avoid using a staff member's personal account, because you will lose control if they leave. If a profile for your business already exists (customers sometimes add businesses to Maps), claim it rather than creating a duplicate.

## Step 2: Get the basics exactly right

**Business name.** Use your real business name as it appears on your signage and documents. Do not add keywords or locations ("Silva Bakery Best Cakes Colombo"). Google's guidelines prohibit this, and profiles that do it can be suspended.

**Category.** The primary category matters more than almost anything else for which searches you appear in. Choose the most specific one that describes your core business, for example "Cake shop" rather than "Store". You can add a few secondary categories for other genuine services.

**Address or service area.** If customers visit you, enter the address and check the map pin carefully. Addresses in Sri Lanka are often informal ("opposite the temple, second lane"), so drag the pin to the exact entrance. A pin in the wrong place sends customers to the wrong street. If you visit customers and do not want your home address shown, set a service area instead and hide the address.

**Phone number.** Use a number someone actually answers during opening hours. If customers mostly reach you on WhatsApp, use that number, since many people will message it rather than call.

**Website.** Link to your website, or to a relevant page on it. If you do not have a website yet, a well-kept profile is still worth having.

## Step 3: Verify

Google needs to confirm the business is real before your profile appears fully in Search and Maps. The available methods vary by business and location, and Google decides which to offer you. They can include:

- a **video recording** showing your signage, location, and proof that you manage the business (equipment, stock, tools of your trade)
- a **phone or text** code
- an **email** code
- a **postcard** with a code sent to your address

Video verification is common now. Plan it before you press record: show the street and the outside of the business with signage, then walk inside and show something only the owner can access, such as unlocking the shop or opening the till or the back office. Keep the video short and continuous.

## Step 4: Complete everything

Completeness helps customers and helps Google understand you.

- **Hours.** Regular hours, plus special hours for holidays.
- **Description.** A plain explanation of what you do and for whom. No links or promotional phrases in capitals.
- **Products and services.** List your main items or services with short descriptions and prices or price ranges where practical.
- **Attributes.** Options such as wheelchair access, delivery, online appointments and payment methods.
- **Photos.** Logo, a cover photo, the outside (so people recognise it), the inside, your products and your team. Real photos taken on a phone in good daylight work better than stock images.

## Step 5: Keep your hours honest, especially on holidays

Nothing frustrates a customer more than travelling to a shop Google says is open and finding it shut. Sri Lanka has a full calendar of public, bank and mercantile holidays, plus a Poya day every month, and many businesses change their hours on those days. Set special hours ahead of time for each one. It takes a few minutes a month and prevents a steady trickle of disappointed customers and bad reviews.

## Step 6: Build reviews the right way

Reviews influence both ranking and whether people choose you. Earn them honestly.

**Ask every happy customer.** Your profile has a short link for leaving a review. Put it on receipts, in a QR code at the counter, and in a thank-you message after a job.

**Never pay for reviews or offer anything in exchange.** Discounts or freebies for reviews break Google's policies, and fake reviews can lead to them being removed or your profile being restricted. Do not ask only satisfied customers while discouraging unhappy ones from reviewing.

**Reply to every review.** Thank people for positive ones. For negative ones, respond calmly, acknowledge the issue, and offer to resolve it offline. Future customers read your replies as much as the reviews.

**Report reviews that break the rules,** such as spam or reviews from people who were never customers, using the reporting option rather than arguing in public.

## Step 7: Keep it active

A profile that is updated regularly signals that the business is real and open.

- **Post updates** about new products, offers and events. A post every week or two is plenty.
- **Add new photos** when you have something new to show.
- **Check for suggested edits.** Anyone can suggest changes to your profile, and occasionally they are wrong. Review them so your information stays correct.

Google retired the built-in Business Profile chat feature in 2024. If customers like to message you, make sure your WhatsApp number or website contact options are clearly listed instead.

## Step 8: Keep your details consistent elsewhere

Your business name, address and phone number should match across your website, Facebook page, Instagram bio and directory listings. Inconsistent details confuse customers and make it harder for Google to trust which information is correct. When something changes, a new phone number or a move, update every listing in one sitting.

## Step 9: Watch what is working

The profile's performance view shows how people find you, which searches you appear for, and how many calls, direction requests and website clicks you get. Check it monthly. If people search for a service you offer but you rarely appear for it, make sure that service is listed and described on your profile and your website.

## A monthly upkeep routine

Once set up, a profile needs about fifteen minutes a month:

1. Set special hours for the coming month's holidays and Poya day.
2. Reply to any new reviews.
3. Publish one or two updates and add a new photo.
4. Review any suggested edits.
5. Glance at performance and note any changes.

Small, steady upkeep beats a perfect setup that is never touched again. For local businesses, it is one of the highest-return fifteen minutes in marketing.
