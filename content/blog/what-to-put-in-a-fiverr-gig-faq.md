# What to Put in a Fiverr Gig FAQ

The FAQ section on a Fiverr gig is easy to ignore. Most sellers fill it with questions nobody asks, such as "Why choose me?" It is better used as a place to answer the doubts that stop someone ordering.

I wrote the FAQ for my YouTube script gig, and this is how I decided what to put in it. I am writing this before my first order, so I can't tell you which questions drive sales.

## Start with the buyer's doubts

Before you write anything, ask what a stranger worries about when they hand money to someone with no reviews. For a writing gig, mine were:

1. Will this be accurate, or will you make things up?
2. Will it sound like me or like a robot?
3. What do you need from me?
4. What if I don't like it?
5. Do you use AI?

Each of these is a reason not to order. The FAQ is your chance to answer them before the buyer has to ask.

## The question I'm most sure about

On my YouTube script gig, one FAQ is: **"Do you use real facts, and can I check them?"** The answer should say that scripts are built from real, public sources, that a source list is included at the higher package, and that the buyer can check every fact against it.

That one matters because factual errors are the biggest risk with scripted video. It turns something a buyer might worry about into a reason to choose me.

## Questions worth writing

- **What do you need from me?** List exactly what you need (topic, tone, length, any facts to include). It also reduces back-and-forth later.
- **How many revisions do I get?** Say the number and what counts as a revision. Vague answers cause arguments.
- **Do you use AI?** Answer honestly. If you use AI tools to help with drafting and structure and then check and edit everything yourself, say exactly that. Hiding it is risky, and some buyers prefer to know.
- **Can you match my voice?** Explain how: for example, by asking for a sample of their writing.
- **How long will it take?** Give a range and say what can change it.
- **What's not included?** Say plainly: for example, uploading, graphics, or keyword research.

## Questions to skip

- "Why should I hire you?" It reads like a pitch, not an answer.
- Anything that repeats the description word for word.
- Questions with a different answer every time ("It depends"). Say what it depends on.

## How to write each answer

1. Start with the answer in the first sentence: yes, no, or a number.
2. Add one sentence of detail.
3. Stop. Two or three sentences is plenty.

## One more thing

Check the FAQ again after your first few conversations with buyers. If the same question keeps arriving in your inbox, it belongs in the FAQ.

## The takeaway

Write your FAQ for the person who is nearly ready to order but not sure. Every answer should remove one reason to hesitate, and none of them should promise something you can't deliver.
